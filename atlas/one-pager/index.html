Atlas Identity — the one-pager

01

What It Is

A portable, self-controlled identity that works across services and networks without being owned by any company. The mesh, exit nodes, and declared-origin routing exist to make that identity demonstrable. They are not the product.

02

The Finding

Destinations block datacenter addresses because they cannot tell whether a person is behind the connection. Network type is a proxy measurement for identity. An identity a relying party accepts removes the need for the proxy.

This inverts the plan. A geographic edge footprint is expensive, scales with the map, and solves a problem the identity layer dissolves. Deferred.

03

The Gap

In one sentence

Atlas has device sessions. It has no long-lived user credential above them.

Found by design analysis, before any relying party integrated. It puts two verified results in conflict:

VerifiedAt which layerWhat it must become
H6 — identity persists across a carrier switch (Bell → Rogers)Mesh: Headscale node-to-user bindingAn identity stable across networks
H5 — one device revoked without disturbing othersMeshAn identity that survives device loss
Declared origin confirmed by TD Bank's fraud detectionRoutingAn attestable origin claim

If relying-party identifiers derive from a device session, revoking a laptop hands the user a new identity at every service. H5 would destroy H6. The properties are right; the layer cannot carry them into the product.

Resolution: the self-held recovery phrase issues device keys. The credential root already exists and already has no company-side copy.

04

The MVP

Not a smaller rewrite. A vertical slice:

Long-lived credential

Above the device layer, rooted in the recovery phrase.

Pairwise relying-party identifiers

Derived from the credential, not the device session.

One relying party, published key

Verified from a published key — not a secret pasted into two demos.

macOS end to end

Sign in, prove this Mac, present identity, declared origin still attestable on existing exits.

H5 and H6 re-run at the credential layer

If revoking a laptop changes the identifier the relying party sees, the MVP failed.

Deliberately out of scope: new mesh, mobile clients, blind-signature issuance, payment, geographic edge.

One or two existing exits stay live. That is attestation infrastructure, not an ISP. Removing them removes the only third-party validation the project has.

06

What Is Built Today

Working mesh, self-hosted coordination server, exit nodes, dashboard, and clients on macOS, Android, and iOS — on the current stack. H5 and H6 verified at the mesh layer. Declared-origin routing confirmed independently by a third party's fraud detection.

Blind-signature issuance, the identity engine, and the payment layer are designed, not built.

07

On Tailscale

Tailscale reached a $2B valuation, acquired Border0 in March 2026 to build privileged access management, and launched Aperture — an identity-linked governance layer where identity is the core organising principle, tying each request and tool call to a known user or workload identity.

Same city, same architectural lineage, and they have moved beyond connectivity into identity-based access control. The overlap is real and should be raised directly rather than avoided.

The vector is opposite. Aperture exists to give security and compliance teams visibility into what a tool did, which resources it accessed, and who initiated it. Border0 logs every session, human or agent, scoped for audit. Tailscale's identity layer makes the person legible to the organization. Atlas makes the person legible to services on their own terms, and unlinkable across them. One builds attribution; the other builds unlinkability.

What their trajectory proves, in both directions. They started at connectivity and had to move up the stack — the mesh alone is not a business. They landed on identity as the layer that matters. Both support this plan. But they reached $2B by serving the relying party, which is evidence about which side of this axis has a buyer.

Implication for scope: the employment identity context stays a reserved placeholder permanently. That is head-on Tailscale territory. Personal and pseudonymous are the defensible contexts.

08

Why the Position Is Defensible

Google, Apple, and the enterprise identity vendors are structurally aligned with the relying party, not the person. That side of the market is unoccupied — partly because nobody has solved paying for it.

Atlas's commitments are structural rather than contractual: no centralized traffic content, self-held recovery with no company-side override, and client-local ephemeral capture with the centralized bootstrap path decommissioned rather than disabled. Each is a capability that does not exist to be compelled, not a policy that can be revisited.
